## Editorial Requirements for All Five Posts

1. **Use real local proof.** Add one verified Kansas City metro project example, original photo set, or owner quote per article. Never invent the city, scope, result, timeline, review, or price.
2. **Be locally substantive.** Include only jurisdiction-specific rules, programs, facilities, fees, or pickup instructions verified from current official sources. Add the source and “last checked” date.
3. **Avoid city stuffing.** Mention a city only where the content changes or where a verified project example exists. Link to city landing pages instead of repeating generic city paragraphs.
4. **Do not publish unsupported prices.** If the business does not approve a price range, explain quote factors and sample job scopes. Competitor prices are research evidence, not Kidd's pricing.
5. **Give every article one owner URL.** The post targets research intent; the service page targets hiring intent. Use descriptive internal anchors toward the service page.
6. **Add FAQ schema only for visible FAQs.** Article schema and breadcrumbs should also match the rendered page.
7. **Use a strong local CTA.** Call, text, or quote request should appear after the short answer and again after the final FAQ.
8. **Make authorship credible.** Name the reviewer/owner and include a reviewed date, particularly on demolition, lead, disposal, and water-safety content.
9. **Refresh annually or when rules change.** Cost/disposal guides should show a year only if there is an assigned refresh workflow.

## Volume Validation Gate

Before assigning a writer, export the following from Google Keyword Planner or the current SE Ranking project for the Kansas City DMA (or the tightest available local radius):

- exact keyword;
- average monthly searches;
- three-month and year-over-year trend;
- competition or keyword difficulty;
- CPC/top-of-page bid as a commercial-intent proxy;
- SERP features and local-pack presence.

Add the volume of close variants within each cluster, then prioritize by **cluster demand × lead value × current ranking opportunity**. Keep Posts 1–3 unless paid-tool data shows negligible local demand. Between Posts 4 and 5, publish the one with the larger validated cluster first; time the basement article before the metro's wetter/storm season.
